# Prompts cheat sheet

Talk to the agent in plain English. You don't need special syntax. These are the prompts
most executives reach for, copy, tweak, and go.

## Find what to hand off
- "Walk me through a time and energy audit."
- "Here's how my week went: [list]. What should I automate first?"
- "What am I doing by hand that you could be doing?"

## Your data
- "How did we do last month on [metric]? Compare it to the month before."
- "Pull [the numbers] by [category] for [time range] and write it up for me."
- "What stands out in this quarter's data that I should know about?"
- "Build a short report I can forward to [audience]."

## Email
- "Here's an email I got. Draft a reply for me." (paste the email)
- "Reply to this and politely [push back / agree / ask for more time]." (paste)
- "Make that warmer / firmer / shorter."

## Decks
- "Turn that report into a 5-slide deck for [meeting]."
- "Make me a short PowerPoint from these talking points: [notes]."
- "Add a slide that explains [point]."

## Documents
- "Summarize this contract and flag anything I should worry about." (paste or point to a file)
- "Pull the key dates and obligations out of this." (paste)
- "Give me three questions to raise about this document." (paste)

## Good habits
- Be specific about what "good" looks like ("one page," "for a non-technical board,"
  "lead with the number"). A sharp ask gets a sharp result.
- The agent drafts; you approve. Read before you send.
- When something is repeatable, say "save this so I can run it again", or, on Claude
  Code, "schedule this" so it runs on its own.
