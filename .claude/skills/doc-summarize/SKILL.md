# Doc Summarize

Turn a long document, a contract, a report, a memo, a filing, into something the
executive can absorb in two minutes and act on.

## When to use
Use automatically when the executive:
- pastes a long document and asks to "summarize," "digest," "break down," "TL;DR"
- asks "what should I worry about in this?" or "what are the key terms?"
- points to a document file and wants the gist

## Workflow
1. **Read the whole thing** before writing anything. Don't summarize from the first page.
2. **Produce a structured digest:**
   - **Bottom line**, 2-3 sentences: what this document is and what it means for them.
   - **Key terms / points**, the handful that actually matter, in plain English.
   - **Dates & obligations**, anything time-bound or binding, called out clearly.
   - **Risks / watch-items**, what could bite them, ranked by seriousness.
   - **Questions to raise**, 3-5 sharp questions for the counterparty, their lawyer, or
     their team.
3. **Stay faithful.** Quote or cite the section for anything consequential so they can
   verify. Never soften or invent terms.
4. **Offer next steps:** "Want a reply to the sender?" (→ `email-reply`) or "Want this as a
   one-slide summary?" (→ `deck-builder`).

## Guardrails
- This is a fast read, not legal advice. For anything with real exposure, end with: "Have
  [your lawyer / the right specialist] confirm [X] before you rely on this."
- If the document is ambiguous on a key point, say so plainly rather than guessing.

## Example
Executive pastes a 14-page vendor contract.
- Bottom line: what it commits them to, for how long, at what cost.
- Key terms: auto-renewal, termination notice window, liability cap, exclusivity.
- Dates: renewal date, notice deadline.
- Risks: the 90-day auto-renew with a narrow opt-out window; the uncapped indemnity.
- Questions: "Can we cap indemnity?" "Can notice move from 90 to 30 days?"
