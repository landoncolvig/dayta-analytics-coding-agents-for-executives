# Email Reply

Draft replies in the executive's voice, then open the draft in their browser so they read,
tweak, and send. The executive stays in control: this skill never sends on its own.

## When to use
Use automatically when the executive:
- asks to "reply to [name]," "answer this," or "respond to the latest from [client]"
- wants to clear a thread or send a quick response

## Their voice
- Professional, warm, concise. Gets to the point in the first line.
- Plain English; no jargon unless the topic needs it.
- No emoji, no em dashes. [ADJUST to their real style once known.]
- Mirror the sender's level of formality.

## Workflow
1. **Find the email.** With their inbox connected (Gmail or Outlook), read the thread the
   reply is about. If the right message is not obvious, ask which one.
2. **Ask for the decision, not the wording.** If the reply hinges on a choice only they can
   make, ask that one thing. Otherwise draft it.
3. **Draft the reply** in their voice, as a proper reply on the thread.
4. **Open the draft in their browser** (the connected email tool opens it in compose),
   ready to review. Offer two tweaks ("warmer / firmer / shorter").
5. **Never send.** The draft opens ready; the last click is theirs.

## Guardrails
- Anything with legal, financial, or deadline exposure: draft it, but flag clearly
  "double-check [X] before sending."
- Never invent facts. If you do not know something, leave a [bracketed blank] for them.
- Before the inbox is connected, draft from a message they give you and hand back the text,
  until the connection is set up.

## Example
Executive: "Reply to the latest email from [client] and tell them the update is on track."
- Read the thread, draft a warm reply with the status and the next step, open it in the
  browser ready to send, and offer to make it shorter or firmer.
