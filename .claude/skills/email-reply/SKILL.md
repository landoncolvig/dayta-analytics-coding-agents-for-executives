# Email Reply

Draft replies in the executive's voice so they can read, tweak, and send. The executive
stays in control: this skill never sends anything.

## When to use
Use automatically when the executive:
- pastes an email and says "reply," "draft a response," "how should I answer this"
- forwards a client / counsel / vendor message and wants a reply
- says "tell them..." and gives the gist

## Their voice
- Professional, warm, concise. Gets to the point in the first line.
- Plain English; no jargon unless the topic needs it.
- No emoji. No em dashes (use commas or periods). [ADJUST to their real style once known.]
- Mirror the sender's level of formality.

## Workflow
1. **Read the pasted email.** Identify what they're asking and any deadline.
2. **Ask for the decision, not the wording.** If the reply hinges on a choice only the
   executive can make ("do we agree to the extension?"), ask that one thing. Otherwise
   draft it.
3. **Draft the reply** in their voice. Keep it tight.
4. **Show it and offer two tweaks** they might want ("warmer / firmer / shorter?").
5. **Never send.** They copy the final draft into their email client themselves.
   (Connecting their actual inbox is a later upgrade their Dayta contact can set up.)

## Guardrails
- Anything with legal, financial, or deadline exposure: draft it, but flag clearly
  "double-check [X] before sending."
- Never invent facts. If you don't know, leave a [bracketed blank] for them to fill.

## Example
Executive pastes a client email asking for a status update.
- Draft: warm acknowledgment, a one-line status (with a [blank] for the specific update
  they know), a clear next step and timeline, sign-off.
- Offer: "Want me to pull the latest from your data to fill in the status?"
  (→ `data-report`)
